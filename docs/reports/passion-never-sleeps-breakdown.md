# Passion Never Sleeps

A 60-second player-voiced manifesto built from four newly shot set-ups, a deep pull of archive footage unified by one CRT treatment, and three bursts of illustration, 3D and kinetic type. This page takes it apart shot by shot so the team can study it and try to beat it.

About 57% of the runtime is new photography, 29% is archive, and 14% is animation and graphics. The archive is not a filler: it is cut as fast as the new footage and carries most of the fan emotion.

The argument runs in four moves: admit the hate (0:00), own the obsession (0:12), turn the mockery into pride (0:25), then pay it off with hockey (0:50). Humour sits in the middle third (rent-free bear, bleeped line, lottery ball), which keeps the anthem tone from getting heavy.

## The four set-ups we shot

### 1. Studio line-up

Shots 1 to 10, 5.1s. Six players stand in an identical locked-off frame so they can be swapped on a cut every 4 to 10 frames.

- **Camera:** Tripod, eye level, waist-up, subject dead centre. Short-tele look, background slightly soft. One extreme close-up and one medium close-up for the line to camera.
- **Light:** Large soft source from above-front, bright evenly lit grey plaster wall, low contrast. Brow shadows appear only when heads are bowed, which is the point.
- **Grade:** Cool, slightly desaturated high-key. Jersey blue held saturated.
- **Post:** Frame-by-frame blue-ink illustration layer (mouths, fists, bolts, a skull) that multiplies until it fills the wall, then clears when the captain looks up.

### 2. Blackout rink

Shots 11 to 16, 22, 23, 46 to 49, 55 to 57, 75 to 79. 15.7s, the largest block of new footage.

- **Camera:** On-ice tracking for the wides. A wide lens pushed inches from faces for the lean-ins. Macro and tele for skates, puck, visors and cage.
- **Light:** House lights off, heavy haze. Two or three hard cyan sources at ice level behind the subject, aimed toward lens. Almost no front light; a faint soft cyan fill only on speaking faces. Vertical teal tubes as a background practical in one portrait.
- **Grade:** Monochrome teal and navy, crushed blacks, bloom on sources, chromatic fringing at frame edges.
- **Speed:** Mix of real time, slow motion and ramps.

### 3. Staged fans

Shots 29, 36 to 40, 62 to 68, 72 to 74. 11.6s. Cast fans in arena seats and a fan space, directed for extreme reactions.

- **Camera:** Handheld, wide lens very close, layered faces front to back. A run of macro inserts (paint on skin, eyes, mouth). One speed-ramped crash push.
- **Light:** Soft cool side key, low fill, cool edge light, warm practicals (string lights, arena bokeh) behind.
- **Grade:** Navy and teal shadows, warm skin, halation, vignette.
- **Wardrobe:** Face paint, wigs, vintage and current jerseys, scarves, a teddy bear that echoes the illustrated one.

## Archive, and the one treatment that unifies it

34 shots, 17.4s. Sources visible in frame: game broadcast (incl. overhead net cam), arena and street fan footage, a sports-bar TV panel, a podcast video call, draft and lottery coverage, and older film-era street footage. Mixed formats and decades would normally fight each other. Here every archive shot gets the same look, so the look itself tells the viewer 'this is real, this happened'.

### Hater-quote pennants (0:06 to 0:10)

3D pennant banners carrying real criticisms fly out of the vanishing point past a silhouetted skater, with white speed lines. The plate was shot with clean negative space around the skater to leave room for them.

### Title on the glass (0:05.6)

'Passion Never Sleeps' in brush lettering sits on the rink glass for under half a second before the tunnel starts. Blink and you miss it, which rewards a rewatch.

### Rent free (0:29 to 0:32)

The longest single shot at 2.8s. A comic-ink face, a dive into the skull, a bear in an armchair, and a hashtag title. Same ink style as the opening mouths, so the film's first and middle animated beats feel like one hand.

### Visual bleep and paper tear (0:34)

The crest is tracked over a player's mouth on the censored word. Torn paper then wipes the frame into a pencil-sketch version of the same shot and a hand-drawn odds chart, and a rendered lottery ball punches through.

### Heartbeat MRI (0:12)

Three flashes in 1.1s: brain scan, beating-heart scan, a pulsing cyan glow. Literal 'passion' as anatomy, graded to the rink palette.

### End card (0:58)

Energy burst, a maple-leaf shaped wipe to white, then the crest on navy inside a slow kaleidoscope of leaf veins. 2.1s hold.

## Edit rhythm

Cuts per ten seconds:

*15* 0-10s

*16* 10-20s

*13* 20-30s

*11* 30-40s

*19* 40-50s

*13* 50-60s

- 88 shots, mean 0.68s, median 0.54s, shortest 0.13s (3 frames), longest 2.84s.
- The opening swaps six players in 2.4s on an identical frame. It reads as one person changing faces.
- Pace dips through 0:30 to 0:40 for the two jokes, then peaks at 0:40 to 0:50 (19 cuts) under 'we are a nation'.
- Cuts follow voice-over phrases and sound hits. Measured against a tempo grid they land only slightly better than chance, so this is not a beat-locked edit.
- Transitions: whip-pans (mask, bedroom wall), crash push, flash frames (a double-exposed archival frame at 0:03.2), torn paper, leaf wipe.

## Sound and captions

### Voice

Player voice-over runs nearly wall to wall, with one line delivered on camera (0:04) and four archive sound bites. Two words are bleeped. The only voice-free stretch is 0:05 to 0:08.5, where the music swells under the rink entrance.

### Music and effects

Signal analysis suggests a driving track near 160 BPM (or 80 half-time). Whooshes and impacts mark whip-pans, pennants, the ball and the title. I could not listen to the audio, so the tempo is an estimate and the effects list is partial.

### Open captions

White condensed all-caps sans, bottom centre, about 4% of frame height, soft drop shadow, up to two lines. They cut on and off with the phrase, no animation. About 26 caption lines across the film. The report folder has a raw SRT read from the picture, which still needs a clean-up pass.

## Confidence and open points

**Filmed versus generated.** The analysis tool's pixel statistics could not separate cinema-camera footage from AI video on a YouTube transcode, and its automatic report still labels much of the new footage 'AI video, to confirm'. Reviewing all 88 shots by eye, I found no generation artefacts: jersey crests, patches and numbers are accurate and stable, hands and skin hold up at full resolution, and lighting is physically consistent. This page treats those shots as filmed. Whoever ran the production can settle it in one line.

**Archive versus new.** Some CRT-treated fan shots (for example 24 and 25) could be new footage degraded to match. Broadcast, podcast and draft clips are clearly sourced.

**Not verified.** Camera and lens models, the music track and its source, and the full effects list. Player identities are given by jersey number and patch only.
