# Four references, taken apart

Four films the team likes for composition, image style and voice. Each was run through the same shot-by-shot analysis as Passion Never Sleeps, then reviewed by eye. The goal is to know exactly what each one does, so the new film can keep the Maple Leafs structure and borrow the right ingredients.

## Side by side

| Film | Length | Shots | Median shot | Picture made by | Voice | On-screen text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Passion Never Sleeps** (our structure) | 60s | 88 | 0.54s | Filmed + archive + animation | Players' manifesto, sound bites | Small white condensed captions |
| 1. Blue fisheye reel | 13.5s | 16 | 0.83s | AI-generated video | None, music only | None |
| 2. Keep Thinking (Claude) | 90s | 65 | 1.33s | Documentary footage + black cards | Many ordinary voices asking questions | White serif, in quotation marks |
| 3. Post Scarcity Blues | 170s | 46 | 3.52s | AI-generated video | One singer, blues sermon | Yellow pixel-font karaoke lyrics |
| 4. Kimi Code teaser | 24s | 42 | 0.20s | Stock, archive, 3D and code collage | None, sound design only | Highlighted code snippets |

Two of the four are fully AI-generated, and they are the two with the strongest image style. The other two get their force from editing: one by withholding the picture, one by flooding it.

## Blue fisheye reel

Sixteen generated clips of nature overtaking a glass city, all through one imitated fisheye lens and one colour.

### How it is made

- AI video, image-to-video style: each clip is one slow drift with no internal cut. The scenes are physically impossible (water as sky), yet textured like photographs.
- Every prompt holds three things constant: fisheye distortion, a flash-lit foreground object inches from the lens, and an ultramarine night sky.
- A small logo mark sits at bottom centre throughout.

### Why it works

- One lens and one colour make sixteen unrelated scenes read as a single world.
- Layering is always the same: flower or hand in front, human or grass in the middle, towers bending in at the edges.
- The cut is locked to the music: 142 BPM, one cut every two beats (0.85s), no exceptions.

### Borrow

- The discipline of a single lens-and-palette rule for every generated shot.
- The ultramarine palette sits naturally beside the Leafs film's navy and cyan.
- Strict two-beat cutting for one section, as a contrast to phrase-led cutting elsewhere.

### How it is made

- Documentary, news and home-life footage in mixed formats, some black and white, with a film look. I saw no signs of AI generation, though this copy is only 480x270.
- 24 black cards carry one line of white serif caption each, in quotation marks. Image flashes between them run 0.6 to 1.7s.
- From 0:46 the black drops away and images run continuously and warmer.
- Closes on one 5s wide shot of a figure facing the sea, then two cards.

### Why it works

- Withholding picture makes each flash hit harder and makes the viewer listen.
- The voices are not a narrator. They are many different people, so the questions feel collected, not written.
- The turn at the midpoint is done with colour temperature and rhythm, not with a new line of copy.
- Tagline: "There's hope in hard questions."

### Borrow

- Real public questions and fears about AI as the script's raw material. This is the direct counterpart of the Leafs film's hater quotes.
- Black as a rhythm instrument: two or three one-line cards between bursts.
- A single long held shot before the logo, after a fast film.

### How it is made

- AI video throughout: background lettering collapses into pseudo-text, and every clip is a single slow push of 2 to 9s.
- One finishing pass unifies it: bloom, RGB fringing, soft scanlines and rounded corners, as if filmed off a tube TV.
- Lyrics appear word by word in a yellow pixel font, with the next words ghosted in grey italics and shouted words in capitals.
- The music is probably AI-generated too (132 BPM by measurement). I have not listened to it.

### Why it works

- A recurring face. The film returns to the singer five times and ends on a slow push into his eyes, which gives 46 generated clips an emotional spine.
- World-building through signs: each shopfront is a one-line joke about the future.
- Two opposed neon colours per frame, never more.
- Slow overall (3.5s median) with one 4-second burst of sub-second riot cuts for contrast.

### Borrow

- The tube-TV pass is the same idea as the Leafs film's CRT archive treatment, applied to generated footage.
- One charismatic on-camera voice as the anchor, in place of the players.
- Written signage inside the world to carry jokes and ideas without narration.
- Word-by-word caption reveal for the lines that must land.

### How it is made

- A collage of found and rendered images: photographs, archival drawings, stock nature clips, scientific visualisations, 3D renders and a code editor.
- Almost every object is centred on a seamless white or pure black ground, and the grounds alternate.
- Teal highlighted code snippets are laid over about a third of the shots.
- No voice and no steady music: sound design only, cutting to silence under the logo.

### Why it works

- Pace. 27 of 42 shots are under 0.3s, many only 4 to 6 frames.
- It stays readable because shapes match across cuts (ring, sphere, helix, lattice) and the subject never leaves the centre.
- Structure is slow, flood, hold: 4s build, 17s burst, 2s on the moon, 3s logo.
- The moon appears four times, so the final hold feels earned.

### Borrow

- This is the model for 'more dynamic than the Leafs film': a centred-object strobe section at 4 to 6 frames per image.
- Code as an annotation layer, the futurist version of the Leafs film's drawn mouths and fists.
- A retro computer as the 'archive' signifier.
- Slow, flood, hold as the shape of the last 25 seconds.

## Mapping onto the Maple Leafs structure

A first pass at how each device in Passion Never Sleeps could be re-skinned for an AI and futurism film using what these references do well. This is a starting point for the treatment, not the treatment.

| Leafs device | Futurist counterpart | Taken from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Six players swapped on a locked frame, drawn mouths swarming | People or objects swapped on a locked centred frame, with code and data annotations swarming, then clearing | Kimi, Leafs |
| Hater-quote pennants flying past the skater | Real fears and questions about AI as flying type, or as black cards in quotation marks | Keep Thinking |
| Blackout rink: one colour, haze, hard backlight | One imitated lens and one colour for every hero shot (ultramarine, fisheye, flash-lit foreground) | Blue fisheye reel |
| Archive unified by a CRT treatment | History-of-computing and science archive under one tube-TV pass; generated footage can take the same pass | Post Scarcity Blues, Kimi |
| Players' voice-over with on-camera lines | One recurring on-camera voice as the spine, with collected public voices as sound bites | Post Scarcity Blues, Keep Thinking |
| The jokes: rent-free bear, bleeped line, lottery ball | Satirical signage inside the world; one mixed-media punchline | Post Scarcity Blues |
| Seven highlight cuts, leaf wipe, crest | Specimen flood at 4 to 6 frames per image, a two-second hold on one image, logo in silence | Kimi, Keep Thinking |

**On 'more dynamic'.** The Leafs film runs at 88 cuts a minute. The Kimi teaser peaks near 140 a minute during its flood and still reads cleanly, because subject position and shape are held constant. Speed is safe when one thing stays fixed.

## Confidence and limits

**Made-by calls.** References 1 and 3 are AI-generated with high confidence (impossible physics, pseudo-text, single-move clips). Reference 2 looks like real footage, but the copy is too small to rule out generated inserts. For reference 4, whether individual specimen images are photographs, renders or generated cannot be settled from the file, and the opening computer could be a photograph, a render or generated.

**Audio.** I measured tempo and voice presence from the separated tracks but did not listen. Voice character and the claim that reference 3's music is AI-made are inferences.

**Tool reports.** The per-video reports in the session folder carry my shot notes. Their automatic headline percentages are unreliable for references 2 and 4, where black cards and 4-frame cuts confuse the classifier.
