# First Word: a one-minute film about AI, planned cut by cut

Nov 10, 2026 · exported 11 Nov 2026 at revision 233

## The film in one line

**FIRST WORD** (working title) takes the commonest dismissal of AI, "it's just autocomplete", agrees with it, and turns it into an invitation: it only finishes what somebody starts. Sixty seconds, 24 frames a second, 1,440 frames.

- **The claim.** Nobody predicts the future. Somebody starts it. A machine that finishes sentences makes the first word the scarce thing, and anyone can write it.
- **The image.** A child on a rooftop at blue hour pushes a blinking bar of light into the lens, while a sperm whale passes through a sky that is the underside of an ocean.
- **The turn.** 0:33. A doubter repeats his own objection, quietly this time: "So it is just autocomplete." The builder smiles for the first time in the film. "Yeah. It is."
- **Opening image and first line.** White type on black: *The future is* and a blinking cyan cursor. Four voices finish the sentence with their doubts. The first scripted line comes at 0:04, straight to camera: "Everybody's got a prediction."
- **Final frame.** The same sentence, now finished by the film: *The future is yours to start.* Under it, larger and in italic, a question lands in three parts on the last three clicks: *What will you start?* The cursor then leaves the sentence and comes to rest after the question mark, blinking: it is the viewer's turn. Nothing else is on the card. Sentence and question hold to the last frame.

The structure is Passion Never Sleeps, beat for beat: concede the criticism, show what it fuels, own the insult, widen the team, state the thesis quietly, strike with proof. It runs hotter, at 100 cuts against 88.

### Three choices that may surprise you

1. The film concedes twice ("Constantly." and "Yeah. It is.") and never claims AI is more than a finisher. The concession is the argument.
2. Real events appear only as public-domain footage or as sourced words on black. No licensed clip is used, and nothing that happened is re-staged with generated actors.
3. The ending does not resolve. The sentence is left for the viewer, which is what makes people answer it.

### How the argument moves

1. **Concede.** The doubts speak first, in real voices, and nobody answers them back.
2. **Say what doubt fuels.** A century of confident predictions, and how one of them aged in 69 days.
3. **Take the strongest objection.** "It makes things up." "Constantly. So we check."
4. **Show three things it finished,** each with what the people then did: checked it, had already proved it, said it out loud.
5. **Flip the insult.** "So it is just autocomplete." "Yeah. It is."
6. **Say the thesis quietly.** "It only finishes what somebody starts."
7. **Widen the team.** A grandmother, four strangers, then everyone: "We'll fix it."
8. **Hand it over.** "Somebody starts it." The sentence stays open.

## Edit

The cut sits on a 144 BPM grid at 24 frames a second, so one beat is exactly 10 frames and the minute is exactly 36 bars. There are 100 shots, with a mean of 0.6 seconds. 48 of them last exactly 5 frames.

63 of the 100 shots run under half a second. That stays readable because one thing is always held still: the frame in the line-up, the centre point in the flood, the bar of light in the handoff.

Since the eighth pass the cut is 98 shots, with a mean of 0.61 seconds; 46 last exactly 5 frames and 60 run under half a second. Two frames of five now each hold a pair of pictures (see the eighth pass).

## Sound

The first sound in the film is a relay clicking twice slowly and three times fast. At 0:53 the same pattern returns as a sperm whale's clicks, because that is where it came from: the 1+1+3 coda used by whales off Dominica.

- **Grid.** 144 BPM, 36 bars. One beat is 10 frames.
- **Angel over engine.** A stacked, breathy high voice over sub-heavy percussion. The voice is an original performance. The percussion is built only from sounds made on screen: the relay, the wax pencil, key hits, paper snaps, the Enter key.
- **Breath.** Close inhales sit under the beat from 0:05 and duck on every kick. When the kick leaves at 0:26.7 the breaths stay, and the viewer hears a room full of people.

### Mix

Voice dry and centred. Music ducks 6 dB under every line. Archive passages push the music through a small speaker. Deliver at -14 LUFS integrated, true peak -1 dB.

### Order of work

1. Keys and a spending cap from you. Nothing paid runs before that.
2. Build the animatic from `shotlist.json`: grey boxes, captions and the click track, every cut at its real length. This needs no keys.
3. Pull the public-domain archive and build the scanline pass.
4. Design the voices and generate every line. Done: eleven voices, 35 lines. Lay them against the animatic and check that 132 words fit.
5. Build two grey previz clips in three.js, the wind and the handoff, to fix their camera paths.
6. Make the reference images for places and props. They carry no words and no faces.
7. Generate in draft mode: the whale first, then the line-up, then the hall. Those three decide whether the look works. Enhance only the takes we keep.
8. Build the code layers and the ink against the animatic.
9. Conform at 24 frames a second, upscale to 4K, apply the scanline pass, and grade to the colour rules.
10. Score and mix, then cut the vertical version from the 4K master.
