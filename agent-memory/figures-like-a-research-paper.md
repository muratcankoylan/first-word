When making images that explain how something was made (process boards, making-of figures, anything shared beside the work), set them as figures in a research paper are set: white page, panels lettered a, b, c, tables with three rules and no boxes, real charts with axes, verbatim source lines, and a "Figure N." caption underneath. Not a dark dashboard with a large headline, cyan labels and filled panels.

The user said on 11 November 2026, about four dark process boards I made for the First Word film: "The insights are okay, but the designs are so AI slop. This needs to be done better, like for a research paper or something." The redo in that style (`tools/process_figures.py` in the project) was not sent back.

**Why:** the user's words give the reason: the dark-board look reads as AI slop. My reading: they share these publicly beside a film about AI, so anything that looks machine-templated undercuts it.

**How to apply:** start in the paper style for this kind of image; put the insight in the caption, not in a headline; prefer a table or a chart of real numbers over decorative panels. This is about explanatory figures, not the film's own look (see [[first-word-no-flat-graphics]]).
